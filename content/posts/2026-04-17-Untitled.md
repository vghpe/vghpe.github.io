I've written about my admiration for the creativity at the animation studio GIANAX before, but this time i'm I just just curious about a very particular element. The stars of 1988 OVA Gunbuster!
<!--more-->
The original Gunbuster backgrounds were probably painted with Nicker Poster Color, which is an opaque watercolor similar to gouache. The stars were probably dotted-on or maybe sprayed-on in case of nebula. 

![Mostly they were scrolling with the background, but a few times they parallax and my bet is a transparent sheet was used. ](/images/posts/gunbuster/buster-examples.jpg)

The interesting artistic choice is obviously how colorful they made it, it's pycadelic, and the dots aren't perfectly spherical. It feels 'arty' for lack of better word. 

--- 

In recreating this effect, the tricky part isn't plastering a canvas with a bunch of blobby particles. It's in re-creating the artifact from the analog pipeline (Lenses, Cameras, Film) that gives it the soft and organic look. 

Zooming in on a hi res capture and compressing the tonal range. I can see some o the effect of the optical. Blue tend to push to the right, red bleeds out more. There's bunch of film noise, and haloing.

![Even with the digital artifact we can deduce a lot info from the analogue pipeline](/images/posts/gunbuster/buster_levels.png)

Using blurs, chromatic aberration, a fake noise grain, some film filters we can get pretty close. Claude helped me write the webGL shaders and then it was a matter of dialing in the value to match up with the references.
